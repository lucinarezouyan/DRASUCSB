Why DRAS exists

The problem is not only where someone reports. It is what happens afterward.

Filing a sexual-assault report can be difficult, and making a report does not guarantee that meaningful action will follow. Survivors may face delays, limited evidence, or uncertainty about how their information will be handled.

DRAS is designed to create an additional layer of documentation and support. The platform centers survivor control while preserving information that may become important later.

How it works

Three steps. The survivor stays in control.

01

Document

Privately record details such as date, time, location, recollection, witness information, messages, screenshots, photographs, or other supporting evidence.

02

Review options

Learn about confidential advocacy, medical care, university reporting systems, and law-enforcement options without being forced into an immediate decision.

03

Decide what happens next

Keep the record private, use it later when making a formal report, or choose to share limited anonymous information for possible pattern recognition.

Privacy & accountability

Preserve information without creating a public accusation page.

✓

Private by default

Documentation remains under the user's control unless they choose to share it.

✓

No public guilt determination

DRAS is not designed to publicly identify accused individuals or determine guilt.

✓

Possible pattern awareness

Limited anonymous information could help trained administrators notice repeated locations, similar circumstances, or repeatedly reported individuals.

Support options
